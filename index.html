            How the numbers were made
            • Every council's records (about 1.7 million in all) were downloaded from the NMC Indian Medical Register in November 2026, one registration year at a time; years with more than 10,000 records were split by registration number.
            • The register holds 29 state councils plus the central register of the former Medical Council of India. A doctor registered with more than one council appears once for each; among records with a usable birth date, the share of such repeats is shown on the all-India page.
            • Gender is estimated, never recorded. The most common first names across India (covering most doctors) were labelled woman, man or unclear; rarer names were placed by name endings that point to one gender at least 92% of the time, and second names such as Kaur, Devi or Kumari were used where the first name was unclear. Everything else is Unknown. Expect a few percent of misreads in both directions.
            • Universities were matched to a state or country by name, including common abbreviations and misspellings. "MD Physician" degrees from Russia, Ukraine and similar countries count as foreign basic degrees.
            • Working age is estimated from year of registration (age today ≈ 2026 − registration year + 25), because the register has no active or retired status and few usable birth dates for older doctors.
            • Some councils stopped sending new entries to the national register years ago (Kerala's stop in 2019). For doctors per 10,000 people, registrations since a council's last complete year are projected with a straight line fitted to its previous ten years; the low case keeps them flat at the last five years' average.
            • Maps place doctors by the PIN code in their permanent address, using India Post's PIN directory; a PIN's location is kept only where its post offices fall inside the PIN's own state (Kerala: its own district). PIN areas with fewer than five doctors are left out. State boundaries: DataMeet (MIT licence); Kerala districts: DataMeet (CC BY 2.5 India).
            • Populations: Report of the Technical Group on Population Projections, 2011–2036 (National Commission on Population, 2020), for 1 July 2026; Kerala districts: 2011 census.
            Source: NMC Indian Medical Register, all state councils and the central register, downloaded November 2026; doctor density for the world and other countries from the WHO. Only aggregate counts are published here. The tables behind each chart are in the data folder of this site's repository.
